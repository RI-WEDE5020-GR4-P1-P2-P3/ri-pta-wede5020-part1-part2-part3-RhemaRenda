# FreshBasket Content Research

## Research purpose

This document records the content decisions used to create the FreshBasket Part 1 website. FreshBasket is fictional, so its organisation-specific content is based on the student's original concept rather than claims about a real business.

## Organisation content supplied by the student

- The store will be based in Thohoyandou.
- Community members will be able to hold shares in the business.
- FreshBasket will create suitable employment opportunities for local youth.
- The business will buy stock from local farmers where possible rather than relying only on large supply companies.
- Prices will be researched with local affordability in mind.
- Customers will earn coupons by purchasing selected items and redeem them later.
- Customers should eventually be able to order for collection and delivery.
- The preferred future colour scheme is dark green and cream.

## Target audience research summary

The proposed audience consists of households, students, senior citizens, working people and small local traders in and around Thohoyandou. These users need clear prices, product availability, opening times, locations and collection or delivery information. The site therefore uses direct language, predictable navigation, labelled forms and descriptive headings.

## Content requirements by page

### Home

- Explain FreshBasket's main value: affordable food, community ownership and local support.
- Direct visitors to products, enquiries and contact information.

### About

- Explain the community share model, youth employment and direct local sourcing.
- Present a mission, vision, values and target audience.

### Products

- Organise proposed products into clear categories.
- Explain selected-item specials and coupon conditions without inventing live prices.

### Enquiry

- Support stock, collection, delivery, supplier, shares and employment enquiries.
- State honestly that form processing will be implemented later.

### Contact

- Provide more than one location reference as required by the brief.
- Clearly label all contact details and addresses as fictional.

## Technical research summary

Semantic HTML gives content machine-readable meaning and supports accessibility, search visibility and browser features (Mozilla, 2026). The pages therefore use structural elements such as `header`, `nav`, `main`, `section`, `article` and `footer`. Form inputs use visible labels, and images use descriptive alternative text in line with WCAG's text-alternative principle (W3C, 2024).

The assignment separates development into three parts. For that reason, Part 1 contains HTML structure and content only. Visual styling will be added through external CSS in Part 2. JavaScript validation, order responses and interactive coupon behaviour will be added in Part 3.

## Content risks and controls

- All organisation-specific details are labelled as fictional or proposed.
- No false customer testimonials are included.
- No live product prices are invented.
- Coupon terms are described as proposed and must be finalised before launch.
- Telephone numbers, email addresses and locations are visibly marked as fictional.
- Online order and form processing are not falsely presented as currently functional.
